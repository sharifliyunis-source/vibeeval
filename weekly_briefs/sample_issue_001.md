## Executive Summary

The Middle Corridor — the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route connecting Central Asia, the Caspian Sea, the South Caucasus, and Türkiye — is undergoing a significant structural expansion. What began as a multimodal freight corridor linking Kazakh and Uzbek producers to European markets is evolving into a four-layer connectivity platform, combining transit and logistics modernization, green electricity export infrastructure, conventional energy connectivity, and the early stages of a trans-Caspian digital backbone. This evolution is supported by sustained multilateral development finance engagement, including EBRD, ADB, and IsDB project pipelines across multiple corridor countries. For infrastructure investors, development finance institutions, and regional policymakers, the corridor's expanding scope represents both a commercially significant opportunity and a long-term development priority.

---

## Main Argument

The strategic significance of the Middle Corridor has traditionally been assessed through a transit and logistics lens — focusing on cargo volumes, transit times, and route competitiveness. This framing, while important, understates the corridor's actual development trajectory. Alongside freight, the corridor countries are advancing green energy export infrastructure, sustaining conventional energy connectivity through established pipeline networks, and beginning to explore a digital connectivity layer through fiber optic projects in the Caspian basin. Each layer reinforces the others: physical infrastructure supports digital deployment; energy revenues fund logistics modernization; digital trade facilitation reduces transaction costs across all layers. Taken together, these developments position the Middle Corridor as an integrated Eurasian connectivity platform with long-term commercial and development finance relevance.

## Layer 1 — Transit and Logistics

### Kuryk Port Expansion and BTK Rail Momentum Redefine Corridor Capacity

The transit and logistics layer of the Middle Corridor is experiencing its most significant capacity expansion in a decade. Kazakhstan's Kuryk port on the Caspian Sea, operated alongside Aktau, is undergoing a phased capacity increase with additional ro-ro berths under construction — a development that directly addresses one of the corridor's most persistent operational constraints: Caspian ferry throughput. Early 2024 data suggests Kuryk's expansion is on schedule, with EBRD financing under active consideration for associated infrastructure.

On the rail segment, ADY Express — Azerbaijan's freight rail operator — has expanded block train services on the Baku-Tbilisi-Kars (BTK) line, achieving documented transit time reductions between the Azerbaijani-Caspian terminal and the Turkish border. BTK, the only operational rail connection across the South Caucasus, is becoming a more reliable commercial proposition as frequency and block train volumes increase.

Complementing physical infrastructure upgrades, Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, and Georgia have jointly launched a customs single-window pilot for container shipments — a trade facilitation measure that addresses a structural weakness of the corridor: documentary complexity at multiple border crossings. The pilot, supported by EU and EBRD technical assistance, has the potential to reduce dwell times significantly if scaled.

**What it means:** Simultaneous port, rail, and customs improvements are reinforcing each other, improving the corridor's commercial competitiveness and making it a more reliable option for shippers diversifying their supply chain routing.

## Layer 2 — Green Energy Corridor

### South Caucasus Electricity Interconnection Advances; Caspian Solar Potential Assessed

The green energy layer of the Middle Corridor is moving from framework agreements toward project-level development. Azerbaijan and Georgia have signed a Memorandum of Understanding on electricity export expansion, building on existing grid interconnection infrastructure. The MOU, while non-binding, signals intent to advance the technical and commercial groundwork for a more structured electricity trade relationship — one that could eventually form part of a broader green energy export corridor linking Central Asia to European markets.

Kazakhstan's government has approved a 1 GW solar energy development plan near Aktau, the country's Caspian port hub. A feasibility study examining export connectivity — whether via undersea cable, existing interconnections, or new transmission routes — has been commissioned. The project is at an early stage, and the export feasibility study has not yet been completed. The IFC and IsDB have been identified as potential financing partners, though no investment commitment has been confirmed at this stage.

The most advanced green energy export project in the region remains the proposed Black Sea Submarine Power Cable, connecting Georgian renewable generation to EU markets. While outside the Caspian segment, it would form the European terminus of a potential green energy corridor stretching from Kazakhstan through Azerbaijan and Georgia to the Black Sea coast.

**What it means:** The green energy layer is advancing incrementally — from MOUs toward feasibility studies and early project development. It is a medium- to long-term corridor dimension, distinct from the operational transit and logistics layer, but increasingly credible as a complementary corridor function.

**Sources:**

- Kazakhstan Ministry of Energy (2024). Aktau Solar Development Plan Approval.

## Layer 3 — Fossil Fuel Export Corridor

### BTC and TANAP Sustain Conventional Energy Export Capacity at Scale

The conventional energy export dimension of the Middle Corridor remains its most commercially mature layer. The Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan (BTC) crude oil pipeline — operated by BP, SOCAR, and partners — recorded throughput of approximately 1.1 million barrels per day in early 2024, representing a quarterly high and demonstrating sustained export capacity from the Azerbaijani Caspian shelf to the Turkish Mediterranean coast.

On the gas side, the Trans-Anatolia Natural Gas Pipeline (TANAP) — which forms the eastern segment of the Southern Gas Corridor — continues to transport Azerbaijani natural gas through Türkiye, with volumes trending above 2023 levels. TANAP feeds into the Trans-Adriatic Pipeline (TAP), which delivers gas to Italy and Greece, completing a functioning energy connectivity arc from the Caspian to Southern Europe.

Kazakhstan is also advancing conventional energy export diversification through the Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC) system and exploring swap arrangements and additional export options via Azerbaijan. While Kazakhstan's primary oil export route is the CPC, Aktau port's capacity — currently being expanded — supports alternative Caspian oil movements. Investment in BTC and TANAP maintenance and optimization continues, reflecting the operators' long-term commitment to the corridor's energy connectivity function.

**What it means:** The fossil fuel export layer is the corridor's most operationally established dimension. Steady throughput at both BTC and TANAP confirms the corridor's role as a durable energy connectivity infrastructure — a foundation that supports the investment case for adjacent layers.

## Layer 4 — Digital Corridor

### Trans-Caspian Fiber Optic Cable Project Enters Feasibility Phase

The digital layer of the Middle Corridor is the least mature but potentially the most transformative dimension of the corridor's long-term evolution. The Trans-Caspian fiber optic cable project — which would establish a dedicated undersea digital connectivity link across the Caspian Sea — has entered its formal feasibility assessment phase, with the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) confirmed as a participant. Kazakhstan's Ministry of Digital Development and Azerbaijan's Ministry of Digital Technologies have both acknowledged involvement.

The cable project, if completed, would establish the corridor as a continuous digital infrastructure route from Central Asia through the South Caucasus and Türkiye to European fiber networks. This matters commercially because data connectivity alongside physical transport significantly reduces transaction costs and enables digital trade facilitation across the full corridor length.

In parallel, Azerbaijan and Georgia have signed a digital connectivity cooperation agreement covering fiber network integration at the bilateral level. This agreement, while technical in scope, supports the gradual construction of a digital backbone along the corridor's South Caucasus segment.

The overall investment requirement for the Trans-Caspian cable is estimated at USD 80–120 million. Financing structures remain under discussion, with no lead arranger confirmed. The timeline for construction and deployment is not yet determined.

**What it means:** The Caspian fiber optic cable project is the cornerstone of the corridor's digital layer. It is in early development — but its advancement from concept to formal feasibility assessment represents meaningful institutional momentum. Alongside existing South Caucasus digital cooperation agreements, the corridor is beginning to develop a digital infrastructure dimension that complements its physical connectivity functions.

## Financing and Investment Angle

### EBRD Anchors Corridor Development Finance; MDB Pipeline Remains Active

Development finance engagement with the Middle Corridor is substantial and broadening. The EBRD's recently approved USD 250 million transport corridor modernization package — covering infrastructure across Azerbaijan and Georgia — is the most significant recent financing event. The package, approved by the EBRD Board, targets multimodal transport infrastructure directly relevant to corridor operations. EBRD involvement at this scale signals institutional confidence in the corridor's commercial viability and development impact.

Beyond the EBRD, the ADB maintains an active corridor financing pipeline through its Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation (CAREC) program, with transport and energy projects in Kazakhstan and broader Central Asia contributing to the corridor's upstream connectivity. The IsDB has been identified as a potential co-investor in Kazakhstan's Aktau solar development plan, though no commitment has been confirmed.

Private sector investment is flowing through ADY Express's commercial expansion on the BTK segment — a notably self-financing infrastructure development in contrast to the publicly financed port and customs modernization programs.

The overall picture is one of multi-institution engagement: no single financier dominates, and the blended public-private character of corridor investment suggests resilience in the financing base.

**Key figures:**
- Institution: EBRD
- Instrument: Sovereign loan
- Amount: USD 250 million (Azerbaijan and Georgia transport package)
- Status: Board-approved

**Sources:**

## Why It Matters

The developments documented in this issue collectively advance the Middle Corridor's transition from a single-purpose freight route to an integrated Eurasian connectivity platform. The simultaneous advancement of logistics infrastructure, green energy export frameworks, operational conventional energy pipelines, and the early-stage Caspian fiber optic project — supported by active multilateral development finance engagement — constitutes a structurally significant development story.

For institutional investors and development finance practitioners, the corridor presents an investment thesis grounded in confirmed infrastructure progress, demonstrated energy throughput, and a growing MDB financing base. For regional policymakers, the corridor's multi-layer development offers a framework for connectivity-led economic integration that extends well beyond transit revenues alone.

The corridor's commercial maturation will depend on whether the institutional momentum now visible across all four layers can be translated into binding investment commitments — particularly in green energy export infrastructure and digital connectivity, where the gap between framework agreements and operational projects remains wide.
